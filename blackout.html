Power Outage

Most outages are short. The ones that hurt are long, hot or cold, or hit someone who relies on powered medical equipment.

Guidance summarized: Nov 19, 2026 · What changed

Generator and CO warningNever run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors or in a garage. Carbon monoxide can kill without warning. See the power page for the full safety rules.

Before

  • Charge phones and backup batteries when storms are forecast. Keep a battery or crank radio and headlamps where you can find them in the dark; skip candles.
  • Freeze water in spare containers; it keeps the freezer cold longer and gives you drinking water later.
  • If anyone depends on powered medical equipment, tell your utility ahead of time and plan a backup, such as a battery pack or a place to go.
  • Install battery-backed carbon monoxide alarms.

During

  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed. USDA says a closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours, a full freezer about 48 hours (about 24 if half full).
  • Layer clothing for cold; in heat, stay in the coolest room, drink water and check on vulnerable neighbors.
  • Leave one light switched on so you know when power returns. Unplug sensitive electronics to avoid a surge.
  • Treat downed lines as live. Stay away and report them.

After

  • Discard perishable food that has been above 40°F for more than 2 hours (USDA guidance). When in doubt, throw it out.
  • Restock what you used and note what failed, such as dead batteries or a missing charger.
