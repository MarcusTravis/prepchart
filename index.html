Common questions

What is Prep Chart?

A free, ad-free reference for getting ready for outages, storms, floods and fires. We compare published kit lists, offer a bag-weight calculator, and chart water, food, power and gear, with links to official sources.

How much water should I store per person per day?

Ready.gov's guidance is about one gallon per person per day, for drinking and sanitation, for several days. Heat, illness, pregnancy and nursing all raise needs. The water and food calculator can size a supply for people and pets.

What goes in a 72-hour emergency kit?

Official lists tend to agree on water, food, light, a way to hear updates, first aid, medications, hygiene, copies of documents, some cash and a way to charge a phone. Ready.gov publishes a basic disaster supplies kit and a list of extra items to consider. See the bug-out bag comparison for how those lists differ.

How heavy should a bug-out bag be?

The official lists we compared do not state a packed weight, and we don't invent one. If you have to walk, the bag has to be a weight you can actually carry. The bag builder gives editorial estimates and a rule of thumb to keep a carried pack to about 20% of body weight or less. The Prepared's Level 1 packing list is stated as under 20 pounds.

Can I run a generator in the garage during an outage?

No. Carbon monoxide from generators is odorless and can kill quickly, including through open windows and garage doors. CDC and CPSC guidance is to run generators outdoors only, well away from the house, with the exhaust pointed away from doors and windows. Details are on the backup power page.

How long does stored emergency food last?

It depends on the food and how you store it. Heat shortens shelf life. On our food chart, some freeze-dried pantry foods carry maker claims of up to 25 or 30 years; USDA figures for canned goods are much shorter (high-acid foods roughly 12–18 months, low-acid foods roughly 2–5 years). Rotate what you store.
