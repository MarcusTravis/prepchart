Carbon monoxide and fuel: read this first

Carbon monoxide (CO) from generators, camp stoves, grills and car exhaust is odorless and can kill quickly, including through open windows and garage doors. This is among the most common causes of death after storms and outages.

  • Run generators outdoors only, well away from the house; U.S. CDC and CPSC guidance is at least 20 feet, with the exhaust pointed away from doors, windows and vents. Never in a garage, basement, crawlspace or porch.
  • Install battery-backed CO alarms on every level and near sleeping areas, and test them before storm season.
  • Headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion while a generator runs: get everyone into fresh air and call 911.
  • Never plug a generator into a wall outlet (backfeeding). Use proper extension cords, or a transfer switch or interlock installed by a licensed electrician.
  • Fuel: turn the generator off and let it cool before refueling. Store fuel only in approved, labeled containers, outdoors in a ventilated shed or similar, away from pilot lights, heaters and sparks. Local fire codes may limit how much you can store.
  • Battery power stations produce no exhaust and can be used indoors; keep them dry and ventilated, and follow the maker's charging guidance.

Sources: CDC, CPSC.

Fuel storage notes

  • Gasoline degrades over time, and ethanol-blended gasoline faster. Fuel stabilizers help, but rotate your supply regularly into a vehicle and refill; check the stabilizer's label for its claimed life.
  • Keep containers closed and out of direct sun, off bare concrete if the container is metal and prone to rust, and away from anything that sparks or has a pilot flame.
  • Propane cylinders stay outdoors, upright and secured, never inside a house, basement or vehicle trunk for long periods.
  • Know your local limits. Many municipalities cap the amount of gasoline you can store at home; ask your fire department.
  • Write the date on each can. Plan to burn the oldest first.
