Prep Styles

People prepare in different ways depending on where they live and what they enjoy. None of these is the right one; use what fits.

Minimalist

Carry less and know more: a small kit built around the basics of water, warmth, light, first aid and communication, plus a plan. The appeal is low cost and low upkeep; the trade-off is that you depend on skills and on being near help. See REI's Ten Essentials and Ready.gov's kit list for a starting frame.

Bushcraft

Skills over stuff: using a knife, fire and natural materials to build shelter and manage in the woods. It's as much a hobby as a plan. It needs practice, and fire and knife rules and land-use rules apply wherever you are. Leave No Trace covers responsible use of the outdoors. Our knives and fire charts are a place to start.

Urban

Planning for apartments, transit and city life: shelter in place for most events, a get-home bag for the commute, and attention to neighbors, stairs and elevators. Space is the limiting factor, so storage is compact and rotation matters. Ready.gov's planning pages cover household and communication plans. See the get-home guide.

Homestead

Self-reliance through production and storage: a garden, preserved food, a larger water reserve and often backup power. It costs more up front and takes ongoing work. Preserve food using tested methods; the National Center for Home Food Preservation publishes them. See water and food and power.
